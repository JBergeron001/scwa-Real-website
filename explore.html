Where to see it in St. Charles County

Confluence Point State Park
Natural place

West Alton

Confluence Point State Park

Flood storageHabitatPublic education

The 1,121-acre park sits where the Missouri River meets the Mississippi. It floods often, so the state is letting much of it return to natural floodplain instead of fighting the water.

What to look for: Where the two rivers meet, the water color changes, the restored wetland and bottomland, and the signs that explain both rivers.

Photo: Kbh3rd, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons

Riverlands Migratory Bird Sanctuary & Audubon Center
Natural place

West Alton

Riverlands Migratory Bird Sanctuary & Audubon Center

Flood storageHabitatPublic education

About 3,700 acres of Mississippi River floodplain, with roughly 1,200 acres of restored marsh and bottomland prairie. The Army Corps of Engineers manages the sanctuary, and the Audubon Center explains how the river, the birds and the floodplain fit together.

What to look for: Swans and eagles in winter, the restored marsh, and the river and flyway exhibits at the Audubon Center (free; check its hours before you go).

Photo: Andy Reago & Chrissy McClarren, CC BY 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons

Marais Temps Clair Conservation Area
Natural place

Portage des Sioux area

Marais Temps Clair Conservation Area

Flood storageHabitat

A 918-acre wetland in an old Missouri River oxbow. It is a remnant of the marsh that once stretched across the lowlands from St. Charles toward Alton. A wetland like this soaks up floodwater and filters it slowly.

What to look for: The shallow marsh pools, waterbirds during spring and fall migration, and how flat and low the old riverbed is.

August A. Busch Memorial Conservation Area
Natural place

Weldon Spring area

August A. Busch Memorial Conservation Area

HabitatWater qualityPublic education

6,987 acres of oak woodland, restored prairie, seasonal wetlands and more than 30 fishing lakes, all inside a fast-growing part of the County. It is part of an Important Bird Area that also takes in Weldon Spring and Howell Island.

What to look for: The restored prairies, the wetland edges, the lake shorelines, and the MDC programs held on site. Check MDC for any closed areas before you go.

Weldon Spring Conservation Area & Katy Trail
Natural place

Weldon Spring area

Weldon Spring Conservation Area & Katy Trail

HabitatConnected greenwaysFlood storage

8,398 acres of forest, bluffs and Missouri River bottomland. The Katy Trail runs right through it, connecting this natural area to towns up and down the river.

What to look for: The bluff-top views over the river bottoms, the forested hollows, and how the trail follows the floodplain edge.

Photo: Yinan Chen, Public domain, via Wikimedia Commons

Howell Island Conservation Area
Natural place

Missouri River, near Chesterfield

Howell Island Conservation Area

Flood storageHabitat

A 2,548-acre island in the Missouri River. The whole island went under water in the 1993 flood, and its bottomland forest and wetlands are built to take high water.

What to look for: Bottomland forest and side channels. You can only reach the island by boat, so many visitors view it from the riverbank.

Dardenne Creek & the Dardenne Creek Blueway
Natural place

Riverside Landing, St. Charles

Dardenne Creek & the Dardenne Creek Blueway

Water qualityConnected greenwaysHabitatPublic education

Dardenne Creek drains about 165 square miles of the County. In 2010, after clean-up work, a 6-mile stretch was taken off the state’s impaired-waters list. The Blueway was Missouri’s first water trail of its kind, and the County is extending it upstream toward Bluebird Meadow Park in Dardenne Prairie.

What to look for: Paddle from Riverside Landing, where Dardenne Creek meets the Mississippi. Watch the creek banks, the riffles and the wildlife along the water. The park offers kayak rentals.

Peruque Creek & Lake Saint Louis watershed
Natural place

Wentzville, Lake Saint Louis, O’Fallon

Peruque Creek & Lake Saint Louis watershed

Water qualityFlood storage

A fast-growing watershed that feeds Lake Saint Louis. It now has a nine-element watershed plan prepared with Missouri DNR (2025 draft), which lays out where runoff and sediment come from and what local projects could fix it.

What to look for: Read the plan first, then look at how new neighborhoods, creeks and the lake connect along the Peruque Creek corridor.

New Town at St. Charles
Built example

St. Charles

New Town at St. Charles

Built into neighborhoodsFlood storageWater quality

A walkable neighborhood built around a network of lakes and canals that hold and slow the runoff from more than 700 acres. Its stormwater system is also its main scenery.

What to look for: How the canals and lakes run alongside the streets and homes, and where the runoff flows in from the streets.

Heartland Park & Heartland Lake
Built example

Wentzville

Heartland Park & Heartland Lake

Built into neighborhoodsWater qualityPublic educationHabitat

A city park built around stormwater-smart design: bioswales, rain gardens, native plantings, and a sediment forebay that keeps soil out of the lake. Wentzville Parks manages it with MDC.

What to look for: The signs at the sediment forebay at the east end of the lake, and the bioswales and rain gardens. The boardwalk has been closed for safety, so check with the city before you go.

Wentzville Law Enforcement Center basin retrofit
Built example

Wentzville

Wentzville Law Enforcement Center basin retrofit

Built into neighborhoodsWater qualityPublic education

An ordinary detention basin turned into a planted one, with more than 500 native plants that slow, soak up and clean runoff from the site. It shows how an existing basin can be upgraded instead of rebuilt.

What to look for: The native plantings across the basin floor and how the water moves through them. The city has a video tour on its project page.

MU Extension in St. Charles County
Learning center

260 Brown Road, St. Peters

MU Extension in St. Charles County

Public education

The University of Missouri’s County office. It runs the Extension Master Gardener course and hands-on workshops, offers soil testing, and has specialists in horticulture, Missouri Master Naturalist and community leadership. It also runs 4-H and nutrition and health programs. Call 636-970-3000.

What to look for: The Master Gardener Demonstration Garden on site. A soil test before you fertilize is also an easy way to keep extra nutrients out of runoff.
